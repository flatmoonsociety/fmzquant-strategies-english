## Overview
The combination strategy of double moving average reversal and ATR trailing stop is a very practical quantitative trading strategy. This strategy first uses the dead cross and golden cross formed by the double moving average to determine the market trend and reversal point. At the same time, the strategy will also set trail stops based on the average true volatility to ensure profits while controlling risks.
## Strategy Principle
### Double moving average reversal strategy
The double moving average reversal strategy uses the intersection of fast and slow lines to determine market trends. When the fast line crosses the slow line from top to bottom, a golden cross is formed, indicating that the market has turned from rising to falling; when the fast line crosses the slow line from bottom to top, a golden cross is formed, indicating that the market has turned from falling to rising. The strategy is to go short when the dead cross occurs and to go long when the golden cross occurs.
Specifically, the strategy uses the fast line of the 9-day STOCH indicator as the fast line and the 3-day EMA as the slow line. When close is lower than the previous day's close, and the fast line is higher than 50 and crosses the slow line above, clear the position and go short; when close is higher than the previous day's close, and the fast line is lower than 50 and cross the slow line below, clear the position and go long.
### ATR Trailing Stop Strategy
The ATR Trailing Stop strategy uses average true volatility to set stop loss points. The ATR indicator can effectively reflect the short-term volatility of the market. The strategy sets a trail stop based on the ATR value, and exits with a stop loss when the price trend reverses.
Specifically, the strategy uses 5-day ATR, and the stop loss point is set to close minus 3.5 times the ATR. When the price reaches the stop loss point, the position is closed and the stop loss is closed.
## Advantage Analysis
The combination strategy of double moving average reversal and ATR trailing stop combines the advantages of the moving average strategy to judge trends and reversals, and the advantage of the ATR trailing stop strategy to control risks, making it a very practical strategy.
Specifically, this strategy has the following advantages:
1. Use the dead cross and golden cross formed by the double moving average to judge the turning point of the market trend and accurately judge the reversal signal.
2. Combine with the STOCH indicator to confirm reversal signals and avoid false signals.
3. ATR trailing stop flexibly sets stop loss points according to market fluctuations to maximize profits.
4. This strategy integrates a variety of indicators and technical analysis methods and uses them in combination to make the strategy more robust.
5. The strategic ideas are clear and easy to understand, the parameters can be adjusted flexibly, and the actual operation is easy.
## Risk Analysis
Although this strategy has many advantages, there are also certain risks that need to be noted:
1. The signals generated by the double moving average may have a lag, making it impossible to buy and sell accurately before and after the reversal point. The moving average period can be appropriately shortened or optimized in combination with other indicators.
2. The ATR indicator is insensitive to large market fluctuations and cannot update the stop loss point in time. Consider combining momentum indicators or volatility indicators for adjustments.
3. The combined use of multiple parameters and conditions increases the complexity of the strategy. Improper parameters may result in overly aggressive trading and increased risk. Parameters need to be carefully evaluated and adjusted gradually.
## Optimization direction
Based on the above risk analysis, this strategy can be optimized from the following aspects:
1. Adjust the moving average cycle parameters and shorten the cycle to capture reversal opportunities in advance.
2. Add other indicators to judge reversal signals, such as MACD, KD, etc., to form multiple confirmations.
3. Dynamically adjust the ATR cycle or introduce market volatility, and update the stop loss point in real time.
4. Evaluate the differences between the stock and futures markets and adjust parameters respectively to make them more consistent with the characteristics of the two markets.
5. Add transaction costs and slippage into consideration during backtesting to make the strategy closer to the real trading environment.
6. Consider adding a machine learning model to dynamically optimize multiple parameters.
## Summarize
The combination strategy of double moving average reversal and ATR trailing stop is an efficient and practical quantitative strategy. It combines the dual advantages of moving average to judge market reversal and ATR setting trail stop to control risk. It can reduce unnecessary losses while ensuring profits. The parameters of this strategy are flexible to adjust and easy to operate. At the same time, it can also be expanded and optimized from multiple angles to make it suitable for a wider market environment. Overall, this strategy provides an excellent strategic framework for quantitative trading.

Reversal123(Length, KSmoothing, DLength, Level) =>
    vFast = sma(stoch(close, high, low, Length), KSmoothing) 
    vSlow = sma(vFast, DLength)
    pos = 0.0
    pos := iff(close[2] < close[1] and close > close[1] and vFast < vSlow and vFast > Level, 1,
	         iff(close[2] > close[1] and close < close[1] and vFast > vSlow and vFast < Level, -1, nz(pos[1], 0))) 
	pos


ATR_TrailingStop(nATRPeriod, nATRMultip) =>
    xATR = atr(nATRPeriod)
    nLoss = nATRMultip * xATR
    pos = 0.0
    xATRTrailingStop = 0.0
    xATRTrailingStop := iff(close > nz(xATRTrailingStop[1], 0) and close[1] > nz(xATRTrailingStop[1], 0), max(nz(xATRTrailingStop[1]), close - nLoss),
                         iff(close < nz(xATRTrailingStop[1], 0) and close[1] < nz(xATRTrailingStop[1], 0), min(nz(xATRTrailingStop[1]), close + nLoss), 
                           iff(close > nz(xATRTrailingStop[1], 0), close - nLoss, close + nLoss)))
    pos := iff(close[1] < nz(xATRTrailingStop[1], 0) and close > nz(xATRTrailingStop[1], 0), 1,
    	     iff(close[1] > nz(xATRTrailingStop[1], 0) and close < nz(xATRTrailingStop[1], 0), -1, nz(pos[1], 0))) 

    pos
