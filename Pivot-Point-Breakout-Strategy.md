## Overview
The breakout reversal strategy is a trend following strategy that captures changes in trend by buying stocks above their most recent support point and selling below their most recent resistance point. This strategy is simple and straightforward, suitable for investors who don’t have much prediction about the market and just want to follow the trend.
## Strategy Principle
This strategy determines the nearest resistance and support lines by calculating the midpoint of the highest and lowest prices for a certain number of days. When the price breaks through these key points, it indicates that the trend has changed and you can trade in the direction of the change.
Specifically, the strategy will first calculate the midpoint of the highest price in the past N1 days as the resistance line, and the midpoint of the lowest price in the past N2 days as the support line. In the buying direction, if the highest price of the day exceeds the nearest resistance line, a buy signal is issued. In the selling direction, if the lowest price of the day falls below the nearest support line, a sell signal is issued. Investors can set the values ​​of N1 and N2 themselves to adjust the sensitivity of the strategy.
This strategy is simple and straightforward. It does not require predicting the market. It only needs to track the breakthrough of key points to capture the trend. Buy when the trend breaks through the resistance line upwards, sell when the trend falls below the support line, and follow the trend.
## Advantage Analysis
- Simple and easy to operate, suitable for investors of all levels
This strategy is very simple and intuitive and does not require any forecasting skills. It can be implemented by simply tracking the breakthrough of resistance and support points. This reduces the difficulty of operation and makes it suitable for investors of all levels.
- Effectively capture trend changes and adjust positions in a timely manner
Breakthrough of key points is a recognized trend change signal in the market. This strategy can respond promptly when trends change, adjust positions, and avoid being stuck.
- Customizable parameters to adjust strategy flexibility
Investors can set the number of days to look at the data on the left and right sides by themselves, thereby adjusting the sensitivity of the strategy. Looking at more days makes the resistance and support lines more stable, while looking at fewer days makes the strategy more flexible and sensitive.
- Easy to combine with other strategies and highly flexible
This strategy mainly implements the trend tracking function and can be easily combined with other timing strategies to improve overall returns.
## Risk Analysis
- There is a certain lag
This strategy requires a certain amount of data accumulation to identify trend changes, which may lead to a certain lag in buying and selling points. You need to pay attention to whether the price has reversed but the signal still continues the original trend.
- Risk of possible false breakouts
The market may falsely break through key points in the short term, which requires investors to have certain coping capabilities to avoid being trapped.
- Higher risk of retracement
This strategy follows the trend with a full position, so there is a greater risk of retracement. Investors need to consider their own risk tolerance. You can also reduce the position size appropriately to reduce the drawdown.
- Need to pay attention to transaction frequency control
If the parameter setting is too sensitive, it may lead to excessive trading frequency. Parameters need to be adjusted appropriately to control the number of transactions. You can also set a minimum holding time to reduce the frequency of transactions.
## Optimization direction
- Optimize parameter settings
You can optimize the parameters of N-day highest and lowest prices in the long term through backtesting and find the best parameter combination. Parameters can also be dynamically adjusted based on market conditions, and more sensitive parameters can be set when the trend is obvious.
- Add breakthrough intensity
You can set a minimum amplitude for breakthroughs to avoid being misled by small false breakthroughs. The greater the breakthrough, the greater the possibility of trend conversion.
- Combine filtering with other indicators
You can add other technical indicators such as RSI, KD, etc. If the key point breaks through and the indicator also shows a divergence, the signal will be more effective. Avoid relying solely on signals generated by breakouts.
- Optimize position management
Risks can be controlled by adjusting positions according to market conditions, and stop losses can be set on the hedging side to avoid huge losses. Positions can also be dynamically adjusted based on trend operation.
## Summarize
The breakthrough point reversal strategy tracks trends through simple key point breakthroughs and can be widely applied to various types of investors. The advantage of this strategy is that it is simple and easy to operate and can effectively capture trend changes, but there is also a certain lag, the risk of false breakthroughs and large retracement. The stability of the strategy can be improved through parameter optimization, adding filters, and improving position management. Generally speaking, this strategy is suitable for investors who pursue simple trend following, but the risks need to be controlled.

## Overview

The pivot point breakout strategy is a trend following strategy that buys stocks when price breaks above recent resistance and sells when price breaks below recent support to capture trend changes. This simple and direct strategy suits investors who have no strong market views but just want to follow the trend.

## Strategy Logic

The strategy calculates the mid points of highest price and lowest price over a period as the recent resistance and support lines. When price breaks through these pivot points, it signals trend changes that can be traded on. 

Specifically, it calculates the mid point of highest price over past N1 days as the resistance line, and mid point of lowest price over N2 days as the support line. On the long side, if today's highest price breaks above the recent resistance line, a buy signal is triggered. On the short side, if today's lowest price breaks below the recent support line, a sell signal is triggered. Investors can customize N1 and N2 to adjust the sensitivity of the strategy.

The strategy is simple and straightforward, requiring no market prediction, just tracking pivot point breakouts to capture trends. It buys when uptrend breaks resistance and sells when downtrend breaks support to follow the trend.

## Advantage Analysis

- Simple and easy to operate, suitable for all investors

The strategy is very simple and intuitive, requiring no forecasting skills, just tracking pivot point breaks. This lowers the difficulty of operation, making it suitable for investors of all levels.

- Effectively captures trend changes and adjusts positions accordingly 

Pivot point breakout is a well-recognized signal for trend changes. The strategy can respond in a timely manner when trend changes, adjusting positions to avoid being trapped.

- Customizable parameters to adjust strategy flexibility

Investors can customize the number of days to look left and right, which adjusts the strategy's sensitivity. More days makes pivots more solid, while fewer days makes the strategy more flexible and sensitive.

- Easy to combine with other strategies for versatility

The strategy mainly provides trend following. It can be easily combined with other timing strategies to improve overall returns.

## Risk Analysis  

- Potential lagging effect

The strategy needs some data accumulation to identify trend changes, which may cause certain lags in signals. Need to watch for price reversal while signals still persist in original trend.

- Risk of false breakouts 

Markets may have short-term false breaks of pivot points. Investors need certain skills to handle whipsaws and avoid being trapped.

- Larger drawdowns

The strategy fully follows trends, thus has relatively large drawdown risks. Investors need to consider their own risk tolerance. Can also lower position sizes to reduce drawdowns.

- Need to control trade frequency

Overly sensitive parameters may lead to excessive trading frequency. Need to adjust parameters properly to control number of trades. Minimum holding period can also help lower frequency.

## Optimization Directions

- Optimize parameter tuning

Can backtest and optimize the N days for highest and lowest to find best parameter mix over long term. Can also dynamically tune parameters based on market conditions, using more sensitive settings when trend is strong.

- Add strength of breakout

Can set a minimum magnitude requirement for breakout to avoid minor false breaks. Stronger momentum on breakout signals higher chance of real trend change.

- Add other indicators as filters

Can add other technical indicators like RSI, KD etc. If breakout aligns with indicator divergences, signals are more effective. Avoid relying solely on breakouts.

- Improve position sizing  

Can size positions dynamically based on market conditions to control risk. Hedges can be stopped out to avoid huge losses. Can also adjust size based on strength of ongoing trends.

## Conclusion

The pivot point breakout strategy captures trends simply through pivotal point breaks, suitable for a wide range of investors. Its advantages are simplicity and effectively capturing trend changes, but it also has some lagging issues, whipsaw risks and large drawdowns. Parameters tuning, adding filters and improving position sizing can enhance the strategy's stability. Overall it suits investors seeking simple trend following, but risks need to be managed properly.

var float lastHigh = na
var float lastLow = na

lowPivot = ta.pivotlow(leftLow, rightLow)
highPivot = ta.pivothigh(leftHigh, rightHigh)

f_updateLevels(pivot_) => 
    var float pastLevel = na
    
    if not na(pivot_)
        pastLevel := pivot_
    
    pastLevel
    
lastLow := f_updateLevels(lowPivot)
lastHigh := f_updateLevels(highPivot)

// Validates the time interval

validTrade =  true

// Orders

if high > lastHigh
    strategy.entry("Long", strategy.long, when=orderDirection != SHORT and validTrade)
    strategy.close("Short", when=orderDirection == SHORT)
if low < lastLow
    strategy.entry("Short", strategy.short, when=orderDirection != LONG and validTrade)
    strategy.close("Long", when=orderDirection == LONG)
    
// Plots

plot(lastLow, "Last pivot low", color.red, offset=1)
