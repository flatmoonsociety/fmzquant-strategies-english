## Strategy Overview
The RSI and MACD crossover strategy is a trading strategy based on the relative strength index (RSI) and the moving average convergence divergence indicator (MACD). This strategy uses RSI and MACD crossover signals to identify potential buying and selling opportunities. When the RSI breaks upward from the oversold area and the MACD histogram turns from negative to positive, a buy signal is generated. When the RSI breaks downward from the overbought area and the MACD histogram turns from positive to negative, a sell signal is generated.
The strategy also introduces two exponential moving averages (EMA) as additional confirmation indicators. The crossover of a shorter period EMA (such as the 10-day EMA) and a longer period EMA (such as the 20-day EMA) can also be used to confirm the signals generated by the RSI and MACD. When the price is above the two EMAs, the buy signal is further confirmed; when the price is below the two EMAs, the sell signal is further confirmed.
## Strategy Principle
1. Calculate the RSI indicator: Use the ta.rsi() function of the Ta-Lib library to calculate the RSI value. RSI is a momentum indicator that measures the magnitude of price movement over a given time period. Its value range is between 0 and 100.
2. Calculate the MACD indicator: Use the ta.macd() function of the Ta-Lib library to calculate the MACD line, signal line and histogram. MACD is a trend following indicator calculated from the difference between two moving averages.
3. Calculate the EMA indicator: Use the ta.ema() function of the Ta-Lib library to calculate the 10-day EMA and the 20-day EMA. The EMA is a reduced-weighted moving average that is more sensitive to recent price changes.
4. Define the buying conditions: When the RSI breaks upward from the oversold zone (default is below 40) and the MACD histogram turns from negative to positive, a buy signal is generated. This suggests that prices may be about to rise.
5. Define the selling conditions: When the RSI breaks downward from the overbought zone (default is above 60) and the MACD histogram turns from positive to negative, a sell signal is generated. This indicates that prices may be about to fall.
6. Use EMA as confirmation: When the price is above the 10-day EMA and 20-day EMA, the buy signal is further confirmed; when the price is below the 10-day EMA and the 20-day EMA, the sell signal is further confirmed.
7. Draw signals: Mark buy signals on the chart with green upward triangles and sell signals with red downward triangles.

2. Delayed signal: Since MACD is calculated based on moving averages, there may be a certain signal delay. This may result in missing the best entry opportunity.
3. Shocking market: Under volatile market conditions, RSI and MACD may produce frequent cross signals, leading to excessive trading and capital losses.
4. Lack of stop loss: This strategy does not clearly define stop loss conditions and may bear greater risks when prices fluctuate violently.
