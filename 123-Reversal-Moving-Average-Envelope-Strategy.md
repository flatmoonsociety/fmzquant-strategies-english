## Overview
The reversal moving average envelope strategy is a quantitative trading strategy that comprehensively utilizes two major technical indicators: reversal trading and moving average envelope. It integrates the advantages of the reversal strategy to capture market reversal opportunities and the moving average envelope to determine the trend direction, achieving stable profits.
## Strategy Principle
The strategy consists of two parts:
The first part is the 123 reversal strategy. Its trading signals come from the stochastic indicator KDJ. The specific logic is: if the closing price is lower than the closing price of the previous day for two consecutive trading days, and the 9-day stochastic slow line is lower than 50, a buy signal is generated; if the closing price is higher than the previous day's closing price for two consecutive trading days, and the 9-day stochastic fast line is higher than 50, a sell signal is generated.
The second part is the moving average envelope strategy. It uses the moving average and the two envelopes above and below the moving average to determine the trend. The specific logic is: if the closing price is higher than the upper track, a buy signal is generated; if the closing price is lower than the lower track, a sell signal is generated.
This strategy comprehensively utilizes the above two trading signals. When the 123 reversal and the moving average envelope send out buy signals at the same time, the strategy will open a long position; when both send out sell signals at the same time, the strategy will open a short position. This can filter out some invalid signals, reduce the frequency of transactions and increase the probability of profit.
## Advantage Analysis
- Combine reversals and trends to increase profit probability
The 123 reversal strategy is good at capturing reversal opportunities near key support and resistance. The moving average envelope strategy can accurately determine the trend direction. Use the two together to catch reversals at high-probability locations.    
-Double filtering reduces transaction frequency
The strategy will open a position only when both indicators send signals at the same time. This avoids the interference of too many invalid signals generated by a single indicator, thereby reducing the frequency of transactions and helping to reduce transaction costs.
- parametrizable parameters provide flexibility to the strategy
The indicator parameters in the strategy are all adjustable. Users can choose the appropriate parameter combination according to market conditions and personal preferences to make the strategy more adaptable.

This strategy only conducts long or short unilateral transactions and does not open reverse positions. This simplifies the strategy operation logic and reduces duration risks.

This strategy relies primarily on reversal trades to make profits. When a long-term unilateral trend occurs, the strategy may produce continuous losses.

Although the strategic design of frequent position changes can lock in small profits, too frequent transactions will also increase transaction costs and unexpected risks.

The strategy does not set a stop loss point and cannot effectively control the maximum drawdown. If a major black swan event occurs, the strategy may face huge losses.

- Optimize parameter combination
Optimize parameters through backtesting and simulated trading to determine the best parameter combination and improve strategy stability. A dynamic parameter optimization mechanism can also be designed to make the strategy more adaptable.

Reversal123(Length, KSmoothing, DLength, Level) =>
    vFast = sma(stoch(close, high, low, Length), KSmoothing) 
    vSlow = sma(vFast, DLength)
    pos = 0.0
    pos := iff(close[2] < close[1] and close > close[1] and vFast < vSlow and vFast > Level, 1,
	         iff(close[2] > close[1] and close < close[1] and vFast > vSlow and vFast < Level, -1, nz(pos[1], 0))) 
	pos


MAE(Length,PercentShift) =>
    pos = 0.0
    xSMA = sma(close, Length)
    xHighBand = xSMA + (xSMA * PercentShift / 100)
    xLowBand = xSMA - (xSMA * PercentShift / 100)
    pos := iff(close > xHighBand, 1,
             iff(close <xLowBand, -1, nz(pos[1], 0))) 
    pos
